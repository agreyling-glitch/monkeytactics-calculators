How to use this free text analyzer

Paste or type text into the editor. The word counter updates words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs, and estimated reading time immediately. Adjust Reading Speed to match your audience; no calculate button is required.

Use the Keyword Density table to find repeated terms and their share of the total word count. You can include or exclude common stopwords, select a keyword to isolate it on the distribution chart, or unscramble a selected word with the local word finder.

Review the four readability grade estimates, unigram, bigram, and trigram tables, sentence rhythm, paragraph structure, and keyword distribution to understand how the writing is organized. Download the finished analysis as a plain-text or Markdown report when you need to save or share the results.

How are the counts calculated?

Words are counted by splitting the text on whitespace and trimming punctuation from each token. Hyphenated words like "well-known" count as one word, matching the behaviour of common word processors.

words = text.trim().split(/\s+/).length density = ( word count ÷ total words ) × 100

Reading time uses the formula reading time = word count ÷ WPM, where WPM (words per minute) defaults to 200. Characters includes every character and space; No Spaces excludes all whitespace — useful when checking text against platform limits that ignore whitespace.

Readability, keyword, and phrase analysis

Readability is reported with Flesch-Kincaid Grade, Gunning Fog, SMOG, and Coleman-Liau. Each formula uses a different combination of sentence length, word length, letter count, or estimated syllables, so the scores are best treated as a range rather than a single definitive grade level.

Keyword density shows individual word frequency, while the n-gram tables reveal common one-, two-, and three-word phrases. These measurements can help editors notice repetition and topic gaps, but they are diagnostic signals rather than keyword targets. Clear, useful writing should take priority over reaching a particular density percentage.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How are words counted?

    Words are counted by splitting the text on whitespace and removing leading and trailing punctuation. Hyphenated terms like "well-known" count as a single word, matching the behaviour of Microsoft Word and Google Docs.

  • What is the difference between characters and characters without spaces?

    Characters includes every character plus spaces and line breaks. Characters without spaces excludes all whitespace, which is useful when comparing against publisher or platform limits that ignore whitespace — for example some SMS and metadata limits.

  • How is reading time calculated?

    Reading time is estimated by dividing the word count by a reading speed in words per minute (WPM). The default is 200 WPM. Adjust it to roughly 250–300 WPM for skimmable web content or 150–180 WPM for dense technical material.

  • What do the readability scores mean?

    Each score estimates the U.S. school grade level a reader may need. A score near 8 suggests eighth-grade reading difficulty; lower scores are generally easier. Treat the four scores as a range, not a precise rating, and use several paragraphs for a more useful estimate.

  • What does keyword density mean?

    Keyword density is the percentage of total words that a given word represents. The list shows the most frequent words in your text, with common stopwords (the, and, of, to, etc.) excluded by default so meaningful terms are easier to spot. It is a lightweight indicator — for full SEO work, also consider context, synonyms, and natural language variations.

  • Can I use this counter for text in other languages?

    Yes — the counter works with any Unicode text, including accented characters, non-Latin scripts, and emoji. Word counting accuracy may vary for languages that do not use spaces to separate words (for example Chinese or Japanese).

  • Can I export the text analysis?

    Yes. After you add text, use Export Analysis to download the counts, readability scores, and top keywords as a plain-text or Markdown report.

  • Is my data private?

    Yes — analysis runs entirely in your browser. No text you enter is transmitted to a server or stored by this tool. See our Privacy Policy for details.
