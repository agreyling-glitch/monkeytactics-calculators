How to use the word unscrambler

  1. Choose a dictionary: use ENABLE for North American play, SOWPODS for international play, or Both for the combined word list.
  2. Enter your letters: repeated tiles are counted, so enter each copy available in your rack or letter set.
  3. Describe the board: optionally select a word length, starting or ending letters, a ? wildcard pattern, and letters every result must use.
  4. Refine the search: open Advanced Filters to exclude tiles, require high-value letters, set vowel or consonant minimums, limit scores, or find hooks.
  5. Rank the plays: sort by score, alphabet, length, rack use, J/Q/X/Z, bingo potential, hooks, or pattern strength, then select Unscramble.

Letter frequency still matters: a result cannot use any letter more often than it appears in your input. Spaces, numbers, and punctuation are not accepted. The selected ENABLE and/or SOWPODS word data is compressed and loaded in small chunks only when a search needs them.

Free anagram solver and Scrabble word finder

Use this word finder to solve an anagram, explore possible words from a letter rack, or check a difficult board position. Length sorting keeps words grouped from longest to shortest or shortest to longest; gameplay sorts create one global ranking so the strongest matching play appears first.

  • Board-state controls: combine exact length, starting or ending letters, wildcard patterns, and required letters.
  • Advanced filters: constrain tile types, vowels, consonants, scores, and dictionary-aware hooks.
  • Gameplay sorting: rank words globally by score, alphabet, length, high-value tiles, bingo potential, hook utility, or pattern strength.

Find the highest-scoring Scrabble words

Select Scrabble score: highest first to compare every match in one score-ranked list instead of scanning separate length groups. Scores use standard English tile values before premium squares or blank-tile adjustments.

The high-value filter and sort prioritize words containing J, Q, X, or Z. Seven-letter results carry a Bingo +50 candidate badge, and the bingo sort moves those rack-clearing plays to the top.

Filter words for a rack or board position

Use Starts With and Ends With for known edge letters. Pattern Search treats each ? as one unknown position, so ??A?E finds five-letter matches with A in the third position and E at the end. Fixed pattern letters and required letters still must fit the supplied tile set.

Advanced Filters can remove words containing excluded tiles, require at least one high-value tile, set minimum vowel or consonant counts, and constrain the base tile score. Reset All Filters restores these options without clearing the entered letters or primary dictionary choice.

Find front hooks, back hooks, and S-hooks

A front hook adds one letter before a word to create another valid word; a back hook adds one after it. An S-hook is the common back-hook case where adding S makes a valid word. Hook badges show front and back counts, identify S-hooks, and reveal the exact hook letters on hover.

Hook detection respects the selected ENABLE or SOWPODS dictionary. Because a front hook may begin with any letter, hook filtering and sorting load the complete selected index on demand; ordinary unscramble searches continue loading only relevant chunks.

How are matching words found?

Each dictionary word is reduced to a canonical signature by sorting its letters. The tool groups those signatures by word length, then checks which groups can be built from your available letters.

Dictionary sources and usage notes

The dictionary selector supports separate searches of ENABLE, a public-domain North American word list, and the ISC-declared potch/sowpods 1.1.0 international package, plus a deduplicated union under Both. These word-game lists may include uncommon, historical, regional, offensive, or general-dictionary-absent terms. A result is not a guarantee of acceptance by every edition, region, tournament, or ruleset.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What words can I make with these letters?

    Enter your letters and select Unscramble. The tool returns every matching word it can build without using any letter too many times. Results use your selected dictionary, filters, and sorting method.

  • Does the Word Unscrambler have to use every letter?

    Not necessarily. Results may use some or all of your letters. Repeated letters can only be used as many times as they appear in your input.

  • Can I use this as a Scrabble word finder?

    The tool can help with word-game searches, and every result includes its standard English tile score. It is not an official game dictionary, and word acceptance can vary by dictionary, edition, region, and ruleset. Board multipliers, blank tiles, and premium squares are not included; seven-letter words receive a separate Bingo +50 candidate badge.

  • Which dictionary does the Word Unscrambler use?

    You can search the public-domain ENABLE list, the ISC-declared potch/sowpods 1.1.0 package, or their deduplicated union. These word-game lexicons may contain uncommon, historical, regional, offensive, or general-dictionary-absent terms.

  • Is this an official Scrabble dictionary?

    No. MonkeyTactics is an independent utility and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Hasbro, Mattel, Collins, or Merriam-Webster. SCRABBLE and related marks belong to their respective owners.

  • How are Scrabble scores calculated?

    The displayed score adds the standard English tile value of each letter in the word. It does not include double- or triple-letter squares, word multipliers, or zero-point blank tiles. Seven-letter words receive a separate Bingo +50 candidate badge.

  • How do wildcard patterns work?

    Enter letters for fixed positions and a ? for each unknown letter. For example, ??A?E requires a five-letter word with A in the third position and E at the end. The result must still be buildable from your letters.

  • How do I find the highest-scoring Scrabble word?

    Open Advanced Filters and choose Scrabble score: highest first under Sort Results. Matches are ranked globally by base tile score, with the highest-scoring word first.

  • What are front hooks, back hooks, and S-hooks?

    A hook adds one letter before or after a word to create another valid word in the selected dictionary. An S-hook specifically adds S to the end. Hook filters and sorts can prioritize words with front, back, S, or multiple hooks.

  • Are my searches private?

    Yes. The dictionary and unscrambling logic run entirely in your browser. Your searches are never uploaded or stored.
