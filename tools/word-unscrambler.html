How to use the word unscrambler

  1. Choose a dictionary: select ENABLE for a broad public-domain list geared toward North American word games, SOWPODS for international British and North American vocabulary, or Both for the widest combined results with duplicates removed. Hover over or focus a dictionary option for a short explanation.
  2. Enter rack letters and a pattern: type repeated tiles exactly as they appear. Use ? for a wildcard rack tile and add an optional pattern after /. In the pattern, ? matches one letter and * matches any number of letters, including zero.
  3. Set the main word filters: open Basic Filters, then use Word Length, Starts With, Ends With, Must Include Letters, and Exclude Letters to describe the match.
  4. Refine the results: open Advanced Filters to require J, Q, X, or Z; set minimum vowels, minimum consonants, and a score range; or select Any Word, No Hook, S, Front, Back, or Multi from the Hooks filter.
  5. Choose a sorting method: use the compact Sort Results menu for score, length, or alphabetical order. Select More options… for grouped Strategic and Hook sorts. Choosing an option automatically reruns the current search.
  6. Unscramble the letters: select Unscramble beside the smart input, or press Ctrl+Enter. On a Mac, use Command+Enter.
  7. Inspect a word: hover over, focus, or select a result word to open its dictionary links and automatically load complete front and back hook words with inline Scrabble point values.
  8. Analyze the matches: when results exist, expand Word Breakdown beneath the word list or press Ctrl+B to jump to it.
  9. Save candidate words: select Pick on a result to add it to the private Pick List, where you can compare scores, add notes, or insert it back into the rack.
  10. Reuse a recent search: open History to restore a rack, pattern, filters, and sorting method. On desktop, press Down Arrow in the rack input for a compact recent-search list.

Letter frequency still matters: a result cannot use any letter more often than it appears in your input. The rack accepts English letters, spaces, ?, /, and *; other characters are removed as you type or paste. The selected ENABLE and/or SOWPODS word data is compressed and loaded in small chunks only when a search needs them.

Press Ctrl+I, or Command+I on a Mac, to jump back to the rack input from anywhere on the page.

Reset All, or Ctrl+R, clears the rack, Basic and Advanced Filters, History, Pick List, and current results. A warning with OK and Cancel appears before anything is removed.

Reuse recent searches with private search history

Every successful word search is saved locally with its rack, optional pattern, active filters, sorting method, result count, entropy, and estimated leave value. Select Re-run to restore the same search settings and run the word finder again. Pin useful searches, sort the list by date or rack, show only pinned entries, or delete individual searches.

The word unscrambler keeps up to 50 unpinned recent searches. Pinned searches stay until you remove them. History is stored only in this browser and is never uploaded; use Clear all history whenever you want to remove the complete list.

Build a Scrabble Pick List and compare words

Select Pick beside any result to save that word for closer comparison. The Pick List shows its word length, base Scrabble score, tile rack, and gameplay flags for hook potential, rack balance, high-value letters, and anagram count. Add a private note to remember a board position or planned play, then sort saved words by score, hook potential, length, or tile-value density.

Use Insert to place a saved word back in the rack input, or press Alt+L to toggle the Pick List from the keyboard. Saved words and notes remain only in this browser until you remove them, clear the list, or approve Reset All.

Find the highest-scoring Scrabble words

Select Score: highest first in the compact Sort Results menu to compare every match in one score-ranked list instead of scanning separate length groups. Changing the sort automatically reruns the current search. Scores use standard English tile values before premium squares or blank-tile adjustments.

The high-value filter and sort prioritize words containing J, Q, X, or Z. Seven-letter results carry a Bingo +50 candidate badge, and the bingo sort moves those rack-clearing plays to the top.

Filter words for a rack or board position

Open Basic Filters and use Starts With and Ends With for known edge letters. In Letters to Unscramble, place the rack before / and an optional pattern after it. For example, ABCDE?? / A*E searches the supplied rack for words beginning with A and ending with E, with any number of letters between.

Use Exclude Letters to remove words containing unwanted tiles. Advanced Filters can require at least one high-value tile, set minimum vowel or consonant counts, and constrain the base tile score. Reset All clears these options along with the rack, History, and Pick List without changing the primary dictionary choice.

Understand your rack with Word Breakdown charts

Word Breakdown appears directly below the result list after a successful search. Expand it to compare the rack's vowel-to-consonant ratio, tile value distribution, and entropy; review a word-pattern heatmap; and see the matching word-length and score distributions. The charts are generated only while the panel is open.

The final row estimates board fit, premium-square opportunities, leave value, and bingo potential. These figures are directional rather than exact because the tool does not receive a board grid or current tile-bag state.

Find front hooks, back hooks, and S-hooks

A front hook adds one letter before a word to create another valid word; a back hook adds one after it. An S-hook is the common back-hook case where adding S makes a valid word. The Hooks filter can find S-hooks, front hooks, back hooks, multiple hooks, or any hookable word.

Select No Hook to find words that cannot be extended by one letter at either the front or back in the selected dictionary.

Hover over, focus, or select a result word to open Available Hooks in the definition popup and see complete front and back hook words. Each hook is shown with its base Scrabble score, such as PANE (+6). The complete dictionary index loads only when the lookup window opens.

Hook detection respects the selected ENABLE or SOWPODS dictionary. Because a front hook may begin with any letter, hook filtering and sorting load the complete selected index on demand; ordinary unscramble searches continue loading only relevant chunks.

How are matching words found?

Each dictionary word is reduced to a canonical signature by sorting its letters. The tool groups those signatures by word length, then checks which groups can be built from your available letters.

Compatible words are checked against every active constraint and then ordered by the selected sorting method. All matching and filtering happens locally in the browser.

Dictionary sources and usage notes

The dictionary selector supports separate searches of ENABLE, a public-domain North American word list, and the ISC-declared potch/sowpods 1.1.0 international package, plus a deduplicated union under Both. These word-game lists may include uncommon, historical, regional, offensive, or general-dictionary-absent terms. A result is not a guarantee of acceptance by every edition, region, tournament, or ruleset.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What words can I make with these letters?

    Enter your letters and select Unscramble. The tool returns every matching word it can build without using any letter too many times. Results use your selected dictionary, filters, and sorting method.

  • Does the Word Unscrambler have to use every letter?

    Not necessarily. Results may use some or all of your letters. Repeated letters can only be used as many times as they appear in your input.

  • Can I use this as a Scrabble word finder?

    The tool can help with word-game searches, and every result includes its standard English tile score. It is not an official game dictionary, and word acceptance can vary by dictionary, edition, region, and ruleset. Board multipliers, blank tiles, and premium squares are not included; seven-letter words receive a separate Bingo +50 candidate badge.

  • Which dictionary does the Word Unscrambler use?

    You can search the public-domain ENABLE list, the ISC-declared potch/sowpods 1.1.0 package, or their deduplicated union. These word-game lexicons may contain uncommon, historical, regional, offensive, or general-dictionary-absent terms.

  • Is this an official Scrabble dictionary?

    No. MonkeyTactics is an independent utility and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Hasbro, Mattel, Collins, or Merriam-Webster. SCRABBLE and related marks belong to their respective owners.

  • How are Scrabble scores calculated?

    The displayed score adds the standard English tile value of each letter in the word. It does not include double- or triple-letter squares, word multipliers, or zero-point blank tiles. Seven-letter words receive a separate Bingo +50 candidate badge. Available Hooks also shows the base score beside each complete hook word.

  • How does the smart letter and pattern input work?

    Enter rack letters before / and an optional positional pattern after it. Question marks before the slash are wildcard rack tiles. After the slash, ? matches exactly one letter and * matches any number of letters, including zero. For example, ABCDE?? / A*E finds rack-buildable words beginning with A and ending with E. The input accepts only English letters, spaces, ?, /, and *; other characters are removed.

  • How do I find the highest-scoring Scrabble word?

    Open Advanced Filters and choose Score: highest first in the compact Sort Results menu. The tool automatically reruns the current search and ranks matches globally by base tile score, with the highest-scoring word first.

  • What are front hooks, back hooks, and S-hooks?

    A hook adds one letter before or after a word to create another valid word in the selected dictionary. An S-hook specifically adds S to the end. Use the Hooks pills to find S, Front, Back, Multi, or No Hook results. Open a result's lookup window to see complete hook words and their base Scrabble scores.

  • What sorting options are available?

    The compact Sort Results menu includes score, longest-first, shortest-first, and alphabetical sorting. Select More options… to open grouped Basic, Strategic, and Hook sorts for rack use, high-value letters, bingos, patterns, and hook utility. Selecting a sort automatically reruns the current search.

  • How do I look up definitions and hooks for a result word?

    Hover over, focus, or select a result word to open links for Merriam-Webster, Collins, Wiktionary, Wordnik, Dictionary.com, and Cambridge. The same popup automatically loads complete front and back hook words with inline Scrabble scores.

  • What does Word Breakdown show?

    After a search returns results, expand Word Breakdown beneath the word list to see vowel and consonant balance, tile value distribution, rack entropy, a word-pattern heatmap, and word-length and score distributions. It also includes estimated board-fit, premium-square, leave-value, and bingo figures. The charts are created only while the panel is expanded.

  • Are the Word Breakdown gameplay figures exact?

    Rack and result summaries are calculated from your entered letters and matching words. Board-fit, premium-square, leave-value, and bingo figures are estimates because no board grid or tile-bag state is provided.

  • Does the Word Unscrambler have keyboard shortcuts?

    Yes. Press Ctrl+I, or Command+I on a Mac, to focus the rack input instantly. Press Ctrl+Enter, or Command+Enter on a Mac, to unscramble the current input. Press Ctrl+R to reset the rack, Basic and Advanced Filters, History, and Pick List; focus returns to the rack input. After results appear, press Ctrl+B, or Command+B on a Mac, to expand and jump to Word Breakdown. Press Ctrl+H, or Command+Shift+H on a Mac, to open History.

  • How does recent search history work?

    Each successful search saves its rack, pattern, filters, sorting method, result count, and gameplay metrics in this browser. Open History to rerun, pin, sort, or delete searches. The tool keeps up to 50 unpinned searches, while pinned searches remain until you delete them.

  • What is the Word Unscrambler Pick List?

    The Pick List saves words you want to compare or reuse. Each saved word can include a note and displays its tile score, length, rack tiles, and gameplay flags. Pick List data stays in local browser storage until you remove it or approve Reset All.

  • What does Reset All clear?

    Reset All opens a warning before clearing anything. Select OK to clear the rack input, Basic Filters, Advanced Filters, History, Pick List, and current results. Select Cancel to keep everything unchanged. Your dictionary and sorting selections are preserved.

  • Are my searches private?

    Yes. The dictionary and unscrambling logic run entirely in your browser. History and Pick List data stay only in this browser's local storage and are never uploaded. You can remove individual entries, clear either list, or approve Reset All at any time.
