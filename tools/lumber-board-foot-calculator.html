How to use the Lumber Board Foot Calculator

Enter the board’s thickness and width in inches and its length in feet. Click Calculate Board Feet to calculate lumber volume and estimated dry weight for one board.

Use nominal or actual dimensions consistently. Lumber invoices commonly use board feet, while finished dimensions may produce a smaller physical volume than nominal sizing.

How are board feet calculated?

A board foot is a volume equal to a board 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide, and 1 foot long. The standard formula combines thickness, width, and length.

Board feet = thickness (in) × width (in) × length (ft) ÷ 12
Estimated weight = board feet × 3.0 lb

The weight calculation uses a general 3.0 lb per board foot estimate. Species, treatment, and moisture content can significantly change actual weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is a board foot?

    A board foot is 144 cubic inches of lumber, equivalent to a piece 1 inch thick, 12 inches wide, and 12 inches long.

  • Should I use nominal or actual lumber size?

    Use the dimensions required by your supplier or estimate. Nominal dimensions are common for purchasing; actual dimensions are better for physical volume.

  • Why is lumber weight only an estimate?

    Wood density varies by species, grade, pressure treatment, and moisture. Wet or treated lumber may weigh substantially more than dry stock.
