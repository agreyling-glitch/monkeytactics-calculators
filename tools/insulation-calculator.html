How to calculate insulation for a project

Select Batt for fiberglass or mineral-wool products installed in framed walls, ceilings, or floors. Add each project section in square feet, subtract doors or other excluded areas, then enter the coverage and weight printed on the package. The realtime estimate returns whole packages, net and waste-adjusted area, total weight, material cost, cavity-fit guidance, and a climate-zone planning target.

Select Blown for loose-fill fiberglass, cellulose, or mineral wool. Enter the net attic area, desired R-value, existing insulation depth and material, and waste allowance. The calculator uses the selected coverage chart to return whole bags, blow-to and settled depth, material weight, added ceiling load, and optional material and labor cost.

1. Measure net area

Enter square feet directly or use the built-in length × height/width helper. Batt projects can contain multiple sections and separate exclusions.

2. Match the product label

Choose a manufacturer chart or enter custom bag weight, R-value per inch, settling factor, coverage rows, and package values.

3. Review and document

Compare depth, coverage, cost, and climate charts, save the setup as a local preset, and print a clean project report.

How are batt insulation packages calculated?

The batt calculator totals the net area of every section, applies the selected cutting-waste allowance, divides by the package-label coverage, and rounds up because partial packages cannot normally be purchased. Package weight and optional price are multiplied by the whole-package quantity.

Net area = sum(section area − excluded area)
Packages = ceil[net area × (1 + waste) ÷ package coverage]
Material cost = whole packages × price per package

Batt R-value depends on the labeled product and its installed thickness. The cavity-depth visualization is a planning comparison; do not compress a thick batt into a shallow cavity to force a higher labeled R-value.

How are blown-in insulation bags and depth calculated?

Existing depth is estimated at R-2.5 per inch for fiberglass or unknown material, R-3.7 per inch for cellulose, and R-3.3 per inch for mineral wool. That estimated existing R-value is subtracted from the target. The engine then selects the first manufacturer coverage row that meets or exceeds the additional R-value and uses its minimum bag rate and published depths.

Additional R = max(target R − existing R, 0)
Bags = area ÷ 1,000 × chart bags per 1,000 ft² × (1 + waste)
Added ceiling load = chart bags per 1,000 ft² × bag weight ÷ 1,000

Always satisfy both the minimum installed thickness and minimum bag count on the product label. The calculator shows the exact chart row used so the estimate can be checked against the bag instructions.

R-value, climate-zone, and ceiling-load guidance

R-value measures resistance to heat flow, and higher values provide greater insulating power. The calculator’s location chart uses 2021 IECC planning values published by the U.S. Department of Energy, but local amendments and the complete building assembly can change the requirement. See the DOE insulation and air-sealing guidance.

Manufacturer coverage and R-value information should be checked against the current product fact sheet and bag label. The FTC R-Value Rule (16 CFR Part 460) governs labeling and advertising disclosures for home insulation.

The blown calculator flags loads above USG’s 1.3 lb/ft² sag-prevention recommendation for unsupported insulation over standard 1/2-inch gypsum panels on framing 24 inches on center. That value is not a universal structural or code limit. Verify the actual ceiling board, orientation, framing, fasteners, finish, moisture exposure, manufacturer instructions, and local code.

Insulation Calculator FAQs

  • How many bags of blown-in insulation do I need?

    Bag count depends on net square footage, the selected manufacturer row’s bags-per-1,000-square-feet rate, and waste. The calculator rounds the final purchase quantity up to a whole bag.

  • Does existing attic insulation reduce the amount I need?

    Yes. Enter its average depth and material to estimate the existing R-value. The calculator uses only the additional R-value needed to reach the selected target.

  • What is the difference between blow-to and settled depth?

    Blow-to depth is the initial installed thickness. Settled depth is the expected long-term thickness after settling. Follow the current product label because materials and applications differ.

  • Why must blown insulation meet both bag count and thickness?

    A coverage chart establishes minimum material quantity and minimum installed thickness for its stated R-value. Meeting only one can leave the assembly below the labeled performance.

  • How does the batt calculator handle waste and openings?

    Excluded area is subtracted within each section before the cutting-waste percentage is applied. The waste-adjusted net area is divided by package coverage and rounded up.

  • Can I calculate an insulation product that is not listed?

    Yes. Choose Custom / User Entered, or choose the Customize entry beneath a built-in product to prefill its values, then replace them with the current package-label data.

  • Are saved insulation presets uploaded?

    No. Batt and Blown presets are stored as JSON in this browser’s local storage on this device. They can be loaded later and used to generate a printer-friendly project report.

  • Does the calculator determine code compliance?

    No. Climate targets, cavity guidance, vapor-control notes, and ceiling-load warnings are planning references. Confirm the locally adopted energy and building codes and the complete assembly before installation.
